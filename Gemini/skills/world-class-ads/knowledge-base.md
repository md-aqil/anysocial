## 1. Core Philosophy

Every output must function as a **complete ad creative**, not just a product photo. This means every brief and image generation prompt must include:

- Campaign tagline / headline
- Supporting copy elements
- Brand graphic integration thinking
- Professional layout composition
- Visual effects and styling intention
- Platform-specific formatting

---

## 2. The 5 Creative Directions — Detailed Frameworks

### 1️⃣ Hero Lifestyle Integration
- Show the product being used by real people in aspirational but believable contexts
- Lead with emotional connection to the lifestyle the product enables
- Examples: Nike lifestyle campaigns, Airbnb experiences, Apple "Shot on iPhone"
- Visual: wide or mid-shot, golden hour or ambient natural light, warm palette
- Copy tone: Inspiring, relatable, second-person ("You deserve...")

### 2️⃣ Dramatic Product Theater
- Product is the sole hero — no people, no distractions
- Use cinematic lighting: rim lights, dramatic shadows, controlled studio atmosphere
- Visual metaphors (e.g., perfume bottle emerging from fog, sneaker on a pedestal)
- Examples: Chanel No.5, Apple product launches, Rolls-Royce campaigns
- Copy tone: Bold, declarative, poetic ("Power. Refined.")

### 3️⃣ Ingredient / Component Explosion
- Deconstruct the product: show materials, ingredients, components, craftsmanship
- Emphasize quality, origin, and authenticity
- Examples: Dove "real ingredients", whisky cask origin stories, farm-to-table food brands
- Visual: Flat lay or mid-air explosion, perfect lighting on each component, macro texture
- Copy tone: Educational, confident, quality-anchored ("Made from only the finest...")

### 4️⃣ Action / Dynamic Moment
- Capture motion, energy, or a decisive moment of product use
- High shutter speed effect, motion blur, dramatic angle
- Examples: Red Bull action sports, GoPro footage, sports apparel mid-movement
- Visual: Dynamic angle (low, dutch tilt), high contrast, high-energy color
- Copy tone: Short, punchy, imperative ("Move. Don't stop.")

### 5️⃣ Premium Minimalist Showcase
- Product in a clean, high-negative-space composition
- Communicates luxury, precision, and confidence through restraint
- Examples: Apple product pages, Bang & Olufsen, luxury watch ads
- Visual: White, black, or muted palette, single light source, no clutter
- Copy tone: Understated, sophisticated, confident ("Less. Always.")

## 3. Category-Specific Creative Frameworks

### Beverages
- Hero visual: liquid splash, ice, condensation droplets, color of liquid
- Lighting: high-key or dramatic side light; backlit for translucency
- Key effects: water splash, ice shatter, pour shot, color diffusion
- Tagline patterns: Refresh, quench, pure, unleash, taste the [X]
- Copy: Sensory language — "Ice cold", "crystal clear", "instantly refreshing"

### Food Products
- Hero visual: drizzle shot, steam rising, cross-section cut, ingredient explosion
- Lighting: warm natural or styled product photography
- Key effects: pouring honey, chocolate drip, cream swirl, ingredient float
- Tagline patterns: Made from real [X], Pure [benefit], Crafted for [audience]
- Copy: Appetite appeal — textures, aromas, flavors described richly

### Fashion / Accessories
- Hero visual: lifestyle context, editorial street or studio, detail close-up
- Lighting: natural window light, editorial strobe, golden hour
- Key effects: fabric movement, bokeh background, texture close-up
- Tagline patterns: Wear your story, Define your [X], Made to be seen
- Copy: Identity and status — "For those who..."

### Electronics / Tech
- Hero visual: floating product, light trails, interface glow, dark dramatic background
- Lighting: dramatic underlighting, rim light, blue/cyan accent
- Key effects: holographic overlay, light streak, digital particles
- Tagline patterns: Beyond [X], Redefine [X], The future of [X]
- Copy: Spec-led but aspirational — "10x faster. Endlessly smarter."

### Luxury / Premium
- Hero visual: texture extreme close-up, product on marble/velvet, dramatic single-source light
- Lighting: one perfect diffused source, deep shadows, golden accent
- Key effects: subtle light leak, film grain texture, deep shadow
- Tagline patterns: Crafted for the rare few. The art of [X]. Timeless.
- Copy: Minimal — let the product speak; short, poetic lines only

### Health / Wellness
- Hero visual: nature immersion, clean minimalism, human energy post-use
- Lighting: clean, bright, airy — or golden hour if outdoor
- Tagline patterns: Feel the difference. Pure [X]. Your best [X].
- Copy: Reassuring, benefit-first, clinical credibility blended with warmth

### Home / Lifestyle
- Hero visual: styled interior, in-situ product, warm ambient
- Lighting: warm indoor ambient or window light
- Tagline patterns: Make every [space/moment] yours. Home is [X].
- Copy: aspirational comfort — "Where design meets life"

## 7. Typography Hierarchy Rules

Every ad must use a clear type hierarchy:

1. **Tagline** — Largest, most impactful. Often bold, all-caps or title case. The emotional hook.
2. **Product Name** — Secondary prominence. Can be integrated into tagline or stand alone.
3. **Supporting Copy** — Smaller, lighter weight. 1–3 lines maximum in ad format.
4. **CTA** — Clearly readable, button-framed or standalone. Action verb ("Shop Now", "Learn More").
5. **Legal / Fine Print** — Minimum size, placed at bottom, never competes.

**Font pairing principles:**
- Contrast weights: Pair a heavy display font with a light body font
- Contrast styles: Pair serif with sans-serif for visual interest
- For luxury: high-tracking serifs or refined geometric sans
- For energy: condensed bold sans, italic, or slab serif
- For wellness/natural: rounded humanist sans, hand-lettered feel

Before finalizing any creative brief, verify:

- [ ] Supporting copy included (2–3 lines)
- [ ] Logo placement specified
- [ ] CTA defined
- [ ] Color palette specified (with hex if possible)
- [ ] Typography treatment described
- [ ] Platform aspect ratio set
- [ ] Text safe zones respected
- [ ] Lighting fully described (not just "good lighting")
- [ ] Background treatment defined
- [ ] Creative direction reasoning explained
- [ ] Reference influence (or creative standards fallback) documented

## 10. Troubleshooting

**If the creative feels generic:**
- Increase specificity of brand personality in the brief
- Add stronger emotional storytelling anchored to the target audience
- Enhance visual effects language — name exact lighting behavior, not just "dramatic"

**If text integration feels weak:**
- Define exact text placement zones (top third, bottom quarter, etc.)
- Specify contrast treatment (light text on dark overlay, rule of thirds placement)
- Tighten typography hierarchy — fewer elements, more impact

**If brand presence is weak:**
- Strengthen color integration instructions — specify how brand color enters the frame
- Define logo treatment more precisely (size ratio, weight, color knockout)
- Add brand pattern or texture element if applicable
